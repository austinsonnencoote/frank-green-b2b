Step 1

Create a development store

Build and test in a free sandbox before touching production. Dev stores on Plus are unlimited and don't count toward your production store limit.

1

Go to your Shopify admin

Open the store switcher in the top-left corner of your admin and click Create store.

Your Shopify Admin
https://admin.shopify.com
2

Select Development as the store type

Name it something clear like frank-green-b2b-dev. This store is your sandbox — everything in this guide should be built and tested here before going anywhere near production.

Help — Development Stores
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/add-expansion-stores
Production limit: You currently have 14 Shopify stores, and Plus includes 10 production stores. Dev/staging stores don't count toward this. Austin has flagged this to your CSM Sophie to review.
Step 2

Create your first Company and Location

Every B2B account is a Company. Each Company has one or more Locations — a physical office, store, or region. Pricing, catalogs, and payment terms are set at the Location level.

1

Go to Customers → Companies

Admin Path (replace [store] with your store name)
https://admin.shopify.com/store/[store]/companies
2

Click Add company

Enter the company name (e.g. "David Jones"). Add a Location (e.g. "David Jones — Sydney CBD") with the shipping address. At each location, set:

  • Payment terms — Net 30, Net 60, or pay-now
  • Catalog assignment (after Step 3)
  • Market assignment (after Step 4)
  • Assigned sales rep
  • Order submission mode — draft (rep reviews first) or direct
3

Add a buyer contact

Enter the buyer's email address. Shopify sends them a passwordless login invite — no password required, just an email code. Test the login yourself before onboarding real accounts.

Help — B2B Companies
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/companies
Start small: Create 2–3 test companies manually to get comfortable. Once you understand the structure, use Matrixify (Step 7) to import all your existing wholesale accounts in bulk.
Step 3

Create a Catalog

Catalogs control which products a buyer sees and what they pay. Create one per pricing tier, then assign it to the relevant locations. Customers with multiple catalogs assigned always see the lowest applicable price.

1

Go to Products → Catalogs

Admin Path
https://admin.shopify.com/store/[store]/catalogs
2

Click Create catalog and configure it

  • Name: Use something descriptive — "AU Wholesale Tier 1", "US Retail Partners", "Corporate & Custom"
  • Products: Filter by tag to include only B2B-specific SKUs (e.g. tag all wholesale bulk packs as b2b)
  • Pricing: Set a blanket % discount off DTC price, or override prices per product — or both
  • Volume pricing: Add quantity breaks per product (e.g. buy 12+, price drops)
Payment terms are not set in catalogs. They're configured per Company Location (Step 2). Catalogs only control product access and pricing.
3

Assign the catalog to a location

Go to Customers → Companies → select the company → open the Location → scroll to Catalog → assign it. That buyer now sees those products and prices when they log in.

Help — B2B Catalogs
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/catalogs
Promotions: For time-limited discounts, create a separate "promo" catalog for just those products and layer it on top. Remove it when the promo ends — pricing reverts automatically without touching any account's base catalog.
Step 4

Create B2B Markets

Markets let different types of buyers see a different version of your store — different products, different navigation, different look and feel. Create one per channel or region.

1

Go to Settings → Markets

Admin Path
https://admin.shopify.com/store/[store]/markets
2

Click Add market for each B2B segment

Suggested markets for frank green:

  • Wholesale AU
  • Wholesale US
  • Wholesale UK
  • Corporate & Custom

Set a subfolder or subdomain per market if you want a distinct URL (e.g. frankgreen.com/wholesale-au).

3

Assign locations to their market

Edit each Company Location → set the Market field to the relevant B2B market. That buyer now sees the experience and catalog for their market when they log in.

4

Customise the storefront per market (July / August — front end phase)

Go to Online Store → Themes → Customize. In the top-left dropdown inside the theme editor, select a B2B market. Any changes made here only apply to that market — different hero, simplified nav, no DTC-only promotions. DTC stays completely unchanged.

Help — Theme customisation per market
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/markets
Help — B2B Markets
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/markets
Step 5

Install Shopify Forms for wholesale sign-up

Shopify Forms is a free app. When a retailer fills in the form, they're created in your admin as a Company with status "ordering not approved" — your team reviews and approves them before they can access the portal. Replaces Jotform.

1

Install from the App Store

Shopify Forms
https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-forms
2

Create a Company contact form

Open the app → Create form → select the B2B company form template. Customise fields to capture what you need — business name, ABN/EIN, contact name, role, address, estimated order volume.

3

Embed it or share a direct link

  • Embed: Add to an "Apply for Wholesale" page on your Shopify store
  • Direct link: Send to prospects directly. No need to make it public — useful for trade shows, outbound prospecting, or sending to accounts you want to onboard manually
4

Approve submissions

Go to Customers → Companies. New submissions appear as "ordering not approved." Click the company → Approve → assign their catalog and payment terms. They receive a login email immediately.

Help — Shopify Forms
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/create-marketing/shopify-forms
Step 6

Enable new Customer Accounts

Shopify B2B requires the new customer accounts (passwordless login via email code). You're migrating from legacy accounts as part of the new DTC launch anyway — this is the same switch, just confirm it's enabled on the B2B store.

1

Go to Settings → Customer accounts

Admin Path
https://admin.shopify.com/store/[store]/settings/customer-accounts
2

Switch to New customer accounts if not already enabled

If it says Legacy customer accounts, click to switch. Buyers log in with their email — Shopify sends a one-time code, they enter it, they're in. No password to reset or forget.

3

Brand the login page

On the same settings page, upload the frank green logo and set the accent colour to match your brand. This is what buyers see when they log in.

Help — New Customer Accounts
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/customers/customer-accounts/new-customer-accounts
Help — Migrating from Legacy Accounts
https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/customers/customer-accounts/new-customer-accounts/migrate
For production: When you flip this on the main DTC store, existing customers with saved passwords will be prompted to use email code instead. Give them advance notice.
Step 7

Bulk-import wholesale accounts with Matrixify

Once catalogs and markets are set up, use Matrixify to import all your existing wholesale accounts from a spreadsheet or NetSuite export. Do not do this by hand.

1

Install Matrixify

Matrixify — App Store
https://apps.shopify.com/excel-export-import
2

Download the Companies import template

Inside the app: Import → Companies → Download sample. Map your data to these columns:

  • Company name
  • Location name + shipping address
  • Payment terms (e.g. "Net 30")
  • Buyer email
  • Catalog and market references — use the exact column header names from the Matrixify sample template, not generic labels. Their naming format is specific (e.g. "B2B: Company: Name")
  • Ordering approval status — set to "approved" for existing accounts you've already vetted
Matrixify Docs — Companies
https://matrixify.app/documentation/companies/
3

Run the import in the dev store first

Matrixify shows a preview before creating anything. Verify the output looks right, then run the same file in production.

Coordinate with dev: Before running a large import, confirm the Celigo B2B connector isn't also auto-creating companies — you don't want duplicates from both systems.

Payments

Why frank green should use Shopify Payments for B2B

The DTC/Stripe decision doesn't need to block this. A new feature — business entities per market — lets you run Shopify Payments on the B2B market while DTC stays on Stripe. No conflict. Here's what it unlocks.

Feature Shopify Payments Stripe (third-party)
Vaulted corporate cards ✓ Yes — buyers save card on file at their location ✗ No — Stripe doesn't integrate at the company/location level
Auto-charge on Net 30/60 due date ✓ Yes — Shopify Flow charges the vaulted card automatically when terms come due ✗ No — Shopify can't initiate charges through a third-party gateway
ACH / bank transfer (US) ✓ Yes — buyers pay by direct bank transfer at checkout ✗ No — not available via third-party gateways on Shopify
Invoice-based checkout ✓ Yes — buyers confirm and pay against terms ✓ Yes — draft orders work either way
Option to keep DTC on Stripe while using Shopify Payments for B2B ✓ Yes — business entities per market lets you run Shopify Payments on the B2B market while DTC stays on Stripe. The two operate independently. ✗ No — staying on Stripe means B2B also stays on Stripe, and you lose all of the above features

Why Stripe can't do this: Vaulted cards, auto-charge, and ACH require Shopify to manage the payment relationship end-to-end. Third-party gateways process transactions but don't give Shopify the ability to initiate a charge on a buyer's behalf at a future date. This is a technical architecture limitation, not a commercial choice.

The right comparison for the internal conversation: It's not "Shopify Payments fee vs. Stripe fee." It's "current cost of chasing Net 30 payments manually, rep time spent on admin, and delayed cash collection vs. the marginal basis point difference." For most wholesale operations, the operational saving outweighs the fee gap significantly.

https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/payment-terms

https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/business-entity
